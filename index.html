Pricing

Local vol pays 130 bp more than flat Black-Scholes at the 5-year ATM-forward volSE 0.8 bp.

Flat Black-Scholes at the 5-year ATM-forward vol of 17.75% pays 5.42% SE 0.4 bp. Local volatility prices the down-and-in put, knocked in below 60% of initial at maturity, on the low-strike wing of the smile. Per unit of the flat-vol coupon annuity, the put moves the coupon by +114 bp SE 0.6 and the redemption timing by -68 bp SE 0.1; the change of that annuity moves it by +84 bp SE 0.3, and local vol pays 6.715% SE 0.72 bp: a flat model needs 20.53% SE 0.02% vol to agree, 2.78 points above the ATM-forward level SE 0.02.

Method and checks

On the headline seed, Monte Carlo and PDE agree within two standard errors at 252 steps per year: -0.12 bpSE 0.72.

A Crank-Nicolson PDE on the same local vol prices the coupon at 6.716%, with no sampling error; the headline Monte Carlo is set against it on its own seed. Pooled over the headline seed and 12 independent seeds of 1,600,000 paths, all at 252 steps per year, the coupon is 6.724% SE 0.30 bp, against 6.716% for the PDE; a fit linear in the time step leaves a bias of +0.40 bp SE 0.07 at 252 steps per year, on 1,600,000 paths per grid; that standard error assumes an error linear in the time step, and the 52-step minus 252-step gap measured directly lies above the line's prediction, at a z-score of +2.4.

Beyond Black-Scholes

Richer models teach networks of their own; their coupon gaps to Black-Scholes are shown under two matching rules.

The network of the neural surrogate card below learns a worst-of autocall on three assets under Black-Scholes, which has a flat smile and Gaussian tails. Other Monte Carlo models take its place as teacher, each with its own network of the same architecture and sizes. Their parameters are anchored on the Eurex index-option settlements of 23 October 2026: over 1,246 of those quotes, the implied volatility of flat Black-Scholes misses by 7.45 vol points (RMSE), that of Merton by 1.23, Heston by 0.74 and Bates by 0.38; the CEV model is anchored on the calibrated surface instead, not fitted to these quotes. A fifth teacher, with a calm and a crisis regime estimated on past stock prices, is part of the project and is kept off this page.

The base scenario is the Black-Scholes point with volatility 25%, correlation 0.50, rate 2.0%, dividend yield 2.5%, protection barrier 60% and maturity 5 years: a note on three stylised stocks, not the note at the top of the page. Each teacher is matched to it in two ways, with the same average variance of the returns as Black-Scholes or with the same at-the-money implied volatility at maturity; under both, with the same realised correlation. A gap depends on what is held equal, which is why both are shown.

Fair coupon: the coupon that sets the model value at 98% of notional; gap: teacher minus Black-Scholes under the same rule, in bp per annum. In brackets, standard errors, Monte Carlo and solved inputs included: bp after a coupon or a gap, percentage points after a probability. Network price error: RMSE in bp of notional of each network against its own teacher on out-of-sample points, net of label noise; the Black-Scholes row is the published network. Last column: share of all simulated paths, early redemptions included, on which the worst stock ends below that share of its initial level at maturity, same-variance rule.

Limits: parameters fitted to index options, or to the index surface for CEV, are given to three stylised stocks that share every parameter; a fitted parameter that ends on a bound of its calibration is set by that bound, not by the quotes, as some parameters of the Bates fit are; no teacher has a jump specific to one stock, the risk a worst-of note is most exposed to; each network reproduces its teacher, discretisation included; these gaps are not corrections to the local-volatility price of the note at the top of the page; both files are run by hand, not by the refresh pipeline, and carry the valuation date of the anchors, 23 October 2026.

Neural surrogate
2,285× faster

A network prices a worst-of autocall on three assets in flat-vol Black-Scholes, neither this note nor local vol, in 36 µs: 2,285 times faster than the Monte Carlo pricer run on 595,792 paths, the path count at which its test-set standard error equals the network's RMSE net of label noise, 3.6 bp (4.5 bp before netting). Against the 100,000-path reference (14.2 ms) it is 389 times faster, and that reference is less precise than the network: a Monte Carlo standard error of 7.9 bp against the network's 4.5 bp.RMSE in bp of notional on 512 out-of-sample points; times are the best of 7 Monte Carlo and 210 network repeats on a shared laptop. Monte Carlo in float64, the reference pricer, against a float32 network; against a float32 Monte Carlo the factor is 1,617.
